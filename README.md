# Is it an e-bike?

A free phone page that checks one e-bike for one rider. Type where they ride, any US state, county, city or town, and the rider's age, then show it the bike's class label, paste the product link, or type the facts in. It answers a plain YES or NO in English or Spanish, with the law behind every line. In San Diego County it also checks the City of San Diego, Carlsbad, Encinitas, Solana Beach, Del Mar, unincorporated county areas and SDUHSD campuses.

Built for Neighborhood Hacks 2026 (Oct 16 to 23, 2026).

## Why

The City of San Diego could start citing riders under its new e-bike rules on Oct 12, 2026, after 60 days of warnings. Riders under 12 can no longer ride Class 1 or 2 e-bikes there, SDUHSD now admits only tagged Class 1 and 2 e-bikes, and stores sell pedal-less e-motos under "kids e-bikes." Families have to sort out state law, city law and school rules for one specific bike. Guides explain the rules. This gives a verdict for one bike and one rider.

## Where they ride

One search box, "Where do they ride?", finds any US state, county, city, town or community. It reads one file, `public/places/us-places.json`, built from the Census Gazetteer files (public domain), and loads it from the app's own origin the first time the box is used. The file has 41,766 rows: 3,144 counties, 32,071 places and 6,551 towns and townships. The 51 states and DC, the City of San Diego's 52 community planning areas, and 71 everyday neighborhood names that City of San Diego pages give for parts of the City, like Hillcrest, University City, Point Loma, Scripps Ranch and Del Mar Heights, are added in code. The short names the City Council district pages use, like Clairemont and San Pasqual, find their planning areas too. Every result shows its county and state, so Carmel Valley in San Diego cannot be confused with Carmel Valley Village in Monterey County. `docs/PLACES_DATA.md` lists the sources, their dates and how to rebuild the file with `scripts/build-places.ts`.

- A pick maps by its Census code, state and county, never by name alone (`lib/places/resolve.ts`). The five checked cities map to their own records. The City of San Diego's community planning areas, like Carmel Valley and La Jolla, and its everyday neighborhood names, like Hillcrest and Del Sur, map to the City. San Diego County and its unincorporated communities map to the unincorporated county record, except three that are mostly base or reservation land, which get California law only.
- Every other place uses its state's verified law, and the answer says so in plain words, like "City and county rules for Austin were not checked."
- Several places can be picked as chips, all in one state, since one answer covers one state's law. Picking a place in another state starts over there and says so in a status line. Each chip has a remove button named for the whole place.
- Exact names come first, then names that only start that way, so "Boston" lists Boston, Massachusetts before Bostonia in San Diego County, and "Kent" lists Kent, Washington before Kentucky. A checked San Diego place comes first among names that match the same way.
- The search is an ARIA 1.2 combobox. The arrow keys move through the results and Enter picks the highlighted one. With none highlighted, Enter picks the one place the name can only mean in the state the answer uses, like Encinitas, or else highlights the first result, so a place the family hasn't seen is never picked for them, and one key press never moves a San Diego ride to Miramar, Florida. Escape closes the list and then clears the box, and a screen reader hears how many places matched. No match, loading and a failed load are shown under the box too. The list opens again when the box is tapped or focused again, or on a phone's Search key. A state still being typed, like "Encinitas, Cali" or "Austin Tex", and a Saint, Mount or Fort still being typed, like "Sain", keep the results on screen. It works in English and Spanish, and Spanish results and chips name counties in Spanish, like "condado de San Diego".
- Removing a chip moves keyboard focus to the next chip, or with none left, back to the box, or on a phone to the box's label, so the phone's keyboard doesn't pop up. A tap on a chip's remove button right after a pick does nothing, so a quick second tap can't remove the place just added.
- A pick in another state is shown in a status line on the page, like "Added Austin, Travis County and 3 other counties, TX. The answer now uses Texas law.", along with the SDUHSD campus choice it removes.
- The SDUHSD campus stays its own choice, shown when the ride is in San Diego County. A plain state picker stays as a fallback. Old links like the QR flyer's `?state=CA&place=city_of_san_diego,sduhsd_campuses` still work, and map to the same entries as the search.

## How it decides

The AI only reads. A fixed rules table decides.

1. **Reading the bike.** A label photo is read by Tesseract OCR right in the phone's browser, so the photo never leaves the phone. A product link is fetched once on the server. Then a fixed-rules parser pulls out the class, watts, speeds, throttle and pedals. It reports a value only when it finds it written in the text, and it shows the exact words it used. It skips the other products under a heading like "You may also like" or "Frequently bought together", and reads this bike's own spec table again at a heading like "Specifications" or "Product information". It leaves the throttle blank when the text says both "no throttle" and "thumb throttle", and never reads "no pedals" wording as pedals. When the text says the pedals were removed, are missing or broken, aren't needed, are optional or an add-on, or don't ship, or answers "Pedals:" with anything but a clear yes, the pedals answer is left to the family. "No foot pegs" reads as no foot pegs, and optional or mixed foot-peg wording is left to the family. A number glued to a letter, the way OCR prints "l500W" for 1500 W, is never read from its later digits. A number split by a space, like "1 500 W" or an OCR "2 8 MPH", is not read at all, and the family is told to check it. It skips battery, motor, box and payload weights, and a weight listed without or before the battery. A weight given with the battery in the next clause is compared with the first one. When weights or wheel sizes disagree, or a size only appears next to an upgrade kit, it uses none and the family is asked. A trike word in a title or slogan never fills in the number of wheels. If the user isn't sure whether the bike has pedals, an optional photo check can look at a side photo of the whole bike. It runs a free detector, OWLv2, on the phone with WebGPU, downloads 194 MB once and only after the user agrees, and only fills the pedals answer. On a close call it asks instead.
2. **Checking the facts.** The "Check the facts" step shows every fact so the user can fix it before anything is decided, including the wheel size, number of wheels and weight that some state rules turn on. A number out of range, like a 57 typed from a tire's millimeter size, counts as not known.
   - Review rounds kept finding new phrasings the parser misread, so a read fact never decides a YES on its own. Every fact read from a product link, pasted text, a label photo or the pedal photo check gets one soft row: a short mark, "Read from the text" (or "Read by the photo check"), the words it came from, and an "It matches the bike" box. Ticking the box counts as checked, and the mark turns into "Checked against the bike" in the same place and at the same size, so the box never moves under a finger. Changing the fact to another value makes it the family's own, and the box gives way to a line that says so. Putting it back to the value that was read makes it read again, still to check, even if it was ticked before, so a stray change away and back never counts as checking it. The number boxes are text boxes with a number keypad, not number inputs, so a mouse wheel, a trackpad swipe or an arrow key can't change a number nobody typed. In a radio group the arrow keys pick each answer they pass, so the throttle speed is cleared only while the throttle answer is No. Moving off No puts it back as read, still to check. Answering the foot pegs or weight follow-up question makes that fact the family's own, even when the answer matches what was read. The wheel size and wheel count follow-ups show the facts as they are, the same as the facts card, and work the same way. Two different wheel sizes share one box under both sizes. It says "It matches the bike, both wheel sizes" while both are as read, and names the one size still as read once the other is changed. Changing both, together or one box at a time, makes them the family's own. Once the card shows Front and Rear boxes it keeps them, so a size midway through typing, like the "1" of "18", never removes the box being typed in. Every box's name starts with "It matches the bike", the words the bottom line tells the family to look for. The unlock fact reads "It has an unlock or off-road mode", so it describes the bike, not the text. Facts typed on the "Type it in" tab are the family's own from the start. After a read, keyboard focus moves to the "Check the facts" heading, unless the family moved to another box while the label or link was read, like the rider's age or the paste box, so their typing is never cut off. A screen reader hears how many facts were read and that each one needs checking before the answer can say YES. That line is cleared once the family edits or ticks a fact or switches the language, so it is never read out again after it stops being true.
   - A new link, text or label photo is a new bike, so every fact it gives starts unchecked again. A pedal photo call that changes the pedals answer marks it unchecked again, shows the text's words as what the text said before the photo check, and asks the family to tick the new answer. A close call leaves the answer as it was. When that answer was read from text and is still unchecked, the message asks the family to tick it or change it, and focus moves to the pedals box, since picking the answer already shown changes nothing. The photo check's message is picked when the call lands and cleared when the family picks a pedals answer, so a screen reader never hears "the answer above is now no" after they picked Yes. A call that lands after the family picked a pedals answer, or ticked or unticked the pedals box, while the check ran is dropped, and the message says which one they did. A pedals answer picked before the check started is replaced by the photo's call, since the family asked for the check, and the new answer then needs a tick. A call is applied only to the facts on screen as it lands, so a call for the last bike that lands just as a new link or label is read is dropped. The photo check says what it is doing and what it found on one status line that is always on the page, so a screen reader reads out the result and that the new answer needs a tick. The count of facts checked is on a line like that too. Each step of the photo check moves keyboard focus to its first control, the photo input keeps focus while the photo is checked, and a result that needs a tick moves focus to the pedals box, so focus never drops to the page. A family that moved on to another fact while the photo was checked keeps their place. A language switch clears the photo check's message, the same as the read count, so a screen reader never hears an old tick instruction again. An edit or tick is applied only to the facts it was made on. If a new link, label or photo call replaces them before the page redraws, the edit or tick is dropped, so it can never check a new value the family hasn't seen. Nothing is saved, so a reload or a shared link starts with no facts and nothing checked. The logic is in `lib/provenance.ts`, apart from the page, so it is tested on its own. The page's own handlers, state and effects are tested too, through a small stand-in for React's renderer in `tests/hooks.ts`, so a page that drops a tick or keeps an old list of unchecked facts fails a test. That includes the new key each read gives the facts card, which takes the last bike's photo check off the page, and the pedals box tick that drops a photo call still running.
3. **Deciding.** A deterministic TypeScript engine (`lib/rules/`) applies the rules table in `data/rules/`. Every sentence on a verdict card comes from a template in `lib/i18n/verdicts.ts`, in both languages. No model writes legal text.
4. **Answering YES or NO.** Every place card and the bottom line answer a plain YES or NO, never a maybe. Only an allowed card with nothing held back is YES. An allowed card says NO, with the same reason as the bottom line, when the bottom line can't confirm that place: a "not sure" fast-mode answer, a record not read in enacted text, a ride after the state check runs out, or a fact read from a link or photo the family hasn't checked yet. The rule the card rests on stays on the card, under "What the rule says for these facts". If a fact the law depends on is missing, the engine asks a quick follow-up question (wheel size, fast modes, weight), and the card says NO until it is answered. A "Not sure" answer, a law that doesn't clearly cover the bike, or a fact the rules can't confirm is NO too, with a plain reason under it, like "We can't confirm it's legal in South Carolina." It never guesses toward "legal." Inside, the engine still keeps can't confirm apart from not allowed, so the bottom line can say exactly what is missing. Only the words changed, not what the engine decides.
5. **The bottom line.** Under every card, one strict YES or NO covers the whole ride: state law, every San Diego place picked, and the SDUHSD campus if picked, on the ride date. The rule lives in `lib/rules/bottomLine.ts`, apart from the page, so it is tested on its own.
   - **YES** only if every card says allowed, every follow-up question is answered (a "not sure" fast-mode answer counts as open, and a Class 1 or 3 label needs a clear "no throttle"), every rule behind the cards was read in enacted text, the ride is no more than 30 days after the state rules were checked, and the family has checked every fact read from a link or photo against the bike. Every read fact counts, not only the ones a card used, since a wrong fact can change which rules apply. That means statute-verified state rules and `primary_verified` San Diego records. It lists what the rider must still do, merged with duplicates removed, and says it covers streets and bike lanes, since sidewalks, parks, beaches and trails have their own rules. When a picked place's own city or county rules were not checked, the card says so in bold, and so does the sentence a screen reader hears, like "City and county rules for Austin were not checked."
   - **NO, not allowed** if any card says not allowed or not an e-bike. It names each place and the reason. When every such card only says not an e-bike, the line under NO says that instead, since the machine may be legal as another vehicle, like a Vermont motor-assisted bicycle, and the app does not check those rules. A read fact the family hasn't checked never changes this NO, since it only errs toward NO. But the NO can rest on a misread fact, like pedals read as missing, so while any read fact is unchecked the card adds one line under "Still to check" that names each one and says the answer could change once they are checked against the bike, in both languages. The sentence screen readers hear says so too.
   - **NO, can't confirm** if nothing is illegal but something is open, can't be confirmed, rests on rules that weren't read in enacted text, or rests on a read fact the family hasn't checked. The headline is a plain NO. The line under it names the one place the NO rests on, like "We can't confirm it's legal on an SDUHSD campus.", says every place we checked when there are more, or says to check the facts read from a link or photo when that is all that is left. Under What's missing it says exactly what is missing, each item in one short line, like "Tick the 7 facts we read, in step 3.", with the full sentence folded under it, which names each unchecked fact in plain words, in both languages. Each place card shows its own YES or NO, and a card never says YES when the bottom line's NO rests on that card or on a fact still to check. A San Diego place card's YES rests on state law too, so on a ride date past the state check it says NO along with the state card. Uncertainty never becomes a YES.
   - No place is picked unless a QR code or shared link names one, so the places an answer covers are always the family's own pick. If no city or county is picked, a picked place has no checked record, or the state has no local layer, the answer says those rules were not checked, and names the picked places when there are any. There is never a bare YES for an unchecked place.
   - Local rules end on their own dates. The City of San Diego's under-12 ban ends Jan 1, 2029 with the state law behind it, unless the state extends that law. Carlsbad's code gives its ban no end date, so from that day a Carlsbad answer for a rider under 12 is NO, can't confirm, never YES.
   - A place with no under-12 ban in force vouches only for ride dates up to 30 days after its records were checked. A ban can come by ordinance, or by a resolution that takes effect the day it is adopted, but state law (VC 21214.7(e)) first requires a public information campaign of at least 30 days, and each record's city or County news was read for one. For a rider under 12 on a Class 1 or 2, a later date is NO, can't confirm, never YES. That includes the City of San Diego after its ban ends.
   - State law gets the same 30-day horizon. The state table was checked on Oct 16, 2026, so a ride after Nov 15, 2026 is NO, can't confirm until the table is checked again, since a new law could be in force by then. The state card says NO too, with the same line. A rule that says no still says no.
   - The ride date can be today through Dec 31, 2029. The rules table keeps only the rules in force from its check on, so a typed past date gets an error, not an answer, and a device clock that runs behind can't reach a date before the check. "Today" is read again at midnight and whenever the page comes back into view, so a tab left open overnight never answers for yesterday.

The San Diego records are held to a reference standard. On Oct 19, 2026 an independent skeptic pass reread each one against enacted ordinance, code, resolution, board and statute text, and only changes it confirmed from that text went in. Each record in `data/rules/san_diego_local.json` carries a `verification_level` and its `primary_sources` (number, link, effective date and quote). Six places are `primary_verified`. SDUHSD is `primary_partial`. Its helmet rule is in Administrative Regulation 5142, but its class, tag, course, walk-on-campus, passenger-seat and e-moto rules are only on the district's web page, and the Board has not voted on the class, tag and course rules, so a campus answer can be NO, not allowed, but never YES.

The costly mistake is calling a bike legal when it isn't, so the engine is built around that:

- Never "legal" in Alaska, Kentucky or South Carolina, where the law doesn't clearly cover e-bikes.
- The watt line is read per state. A 750 W label passes in the 24 states that say "750 watts or less" and is NO, can't confirm, in the 19 that say "less than 750."
- Every `verdict_cautions` condition in the table is checked, with three-valued logic, so a fact the label can't show becomes a question instead of a guess.
- Rules switch on their effective dates (Hawaii Nov 12, 2026, North Carolina Dec 1, 2026, California, Colorado, Illinois, New Jersey and Oregon Jan 1, 2027, Utah May 5, 2027). Pick a future ride date to see them.
- A YES lists what the rider must still do. That includes a Class 3 speedometer in the 30 states whose records require one, New York's rule to ride only on roads posted 30 mph or less, and a parent or guardian, not any adult, for a Utah rider 8 to 13.
- Class 2 verdicts carry a caution in the 18 states whose law also says the motor must stop when the rider stops pedaling. Reviewers kept Class 2 legal there, and so does this app, but no court has settled it.

## Accuracy

See `RESULTS.md` and the in-app accuracy page, in English at `/accuracy` and Spanish at `/accuracy/es`. The headline: on 516,200 generated cases (every state, class, age band, caution condition and switch date, plus bikes with pedals unknown or missing, no class, two classes, facts that disagree, a throttle answered "not sure" with a throttle speed still stated, a throttle left blank, or a top speed of unknown mode), checked against an independent reading of the raw table in `tests/oracle.ts`, the engine gave **zero false-legal results** and never refused a case the table allows.

The bottom line has its own gates. On 605,200 generated rides, including every San Diego place alone, all of them and none, with and without a place whose own rules were not checked, each run four ways (typed in, and read from a link or photo with no fact checked, every fact ticked and then one changed and put back to the value read, or every fact checked), 2,420,800 bottom lines in all, it never said YES when the independent checker listed a reason against it, and never refused when it listed none. It never said YES while a read fact was unchecked, and every NO, of either kind, named exactly the unchecked facts in one line. The matrix bikes hold every fact the app can mark as read, including a peak and an up-to watts figure given next to the rated watts. A NO, not allowed came out word for word the same whether the facts were typed in, or read and checked or not. It said "NO, not allowed" exactly when the checker found a rule that says no. The line naming unchecked places, and the line naming unchecked facts, are checked on the real rendered card, in both languages. Every rendered place card, bottom line and screen reader sentence the matrix reads says a plain YES or NO, in both languages, never a hedge like "Can't confirm", "unsure" or "not sure" in its place. No place card said YES under a NO that rests on that place or on a fact still to check, and every card's YES or NO matched what the raw data gives it, across 3,082 distinct rendered place cards in both languages. An allowed card says NO when the bottom line can't confirm it, like a ride date past the state check, so of the 232,684 allowed cards in the engine matrix, 44,150 say YES and 188,534 say NO, and `RESULTS.md` shows both.

Those tests prove the engine matches the table, not that the table matches the law. The state table was checked against statute text on Oct 16, 2026 in two verifier rounds (`data/rules/verification_report.md`, `data/rules/corrections.json`). The San Diego records were checked against enacted text on Oct 19, 2026, as described above.

The pilot in `PILOT.md` set pass bars before each run. A free CLIP model caught every pedal-less e-moto but called 4 of 8 real e-bikes pedal-less, so whole-bike photos were cut from the app. A second, part-focused try was built on 47 photos and run once on 24 fresh ones. It caught only 9 of 12 no-pedal machines and got 18 of 24 overall against a bar of 22. A third try used OWLv2, a free open-vocabulary detector, with one rule picked on 71 photos. Before its single run on 24 fresh photos, the expected result was written down as 88.7%. It passed every bar, with 12 of 12 no-pedal machines and 23 of 24 overall, so whole-bike photos are back as an optional pedal check, never a verdict.

## Design

One calm column, at most 640 px wide, in four steps: Where and who, The bike, Check the facts, The answer. Steps 3 and 4 show as outlines until they fill. While the page's script loads, a frame of the same shape shows, with the bar, the title, the tagline and the steps in the phone's language, so nothing jumps when the page arrives. A small inline script sets the page's language before the first paint (`lib/pageLang.ts`), which also tells screen readers the Spanish accuracy page is Spanish. The answer step leads with the bottom line YES or NO, then one compact row per place, whose rules, sources and dates open on tap. YES is green and NO is red, and each always comes with its mark and the word. The facts check is a list of each fact, the words it was read from and its "It matches the bike" box, with a bar for how many are checked. On a phone the Check button and the count stay at the bottom of the facts card. Body text is 16 px, tap targets are at least 44 px, source links included, and the light theme is the default, with a dark theme from the phone's setting. On cards "e-bike" and ages like "14-year-old" use non-breaking hyphens, so a line never ends on "e-". Headings and the flyer are balanced, and no paragraph leaves one word alone on its last line. Scrolling to a step is instant when the phone asks for reduced motion. `tests/contrast.test.ts` reads every color pair from `app/globals.css` and checks it against WCAG AA in both themes. The page makes no new outside requests, and Geist is bundled with the app.

## Privacy

No accounts and no database. Label photos are read on the device and never uploaded. The optional pedal photo check also runs on the device, and its model comes once from Hugging Face and jsDelivr. A product link is fetched once and nothing is stored. The listing route refuses private and internal addresses.

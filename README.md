# Is it an e-bike?

A free phone page that checks one e-bike for one rider. Type where they ride, any US state, county, city or town, and the rider's age, then show it the bike's class label, paste the product link, or type the facts in. It answers a plain YES or NO in English or Spanish, with the law behind every line. In San Diego County it also checks 18 cities (San Diego, Carlsbad, Chula Vista, Coronado, Del Mar, El Cajon, Encinitas, Escondido, Imperial Beach, La Mesa, Lemon Grove, National City, Oceanside, Poway, San Marcos, Santee, Solana Beach and Vista), unincorporated county areas and SDUHSD campuses.

Four more parts sit beside the checker, linked from the header.

- **ZIP codes.** The place search also finds all 33,642 US ZIP codes, in the checker, `/mods` and `/find`. A ZIP adds its city only when that city holds at least 99 percent of its land, like 92130 for the City of San Diego. A ZIP split across places, like 92067, lists them so the family picks the exact one, and never guesses.
- **Every school district.** The place search also finds all 19,430 US public school districts. Picking San Dieguito Union High turns on the checked SDUHSD campus record. Any other district is named in the answer, like "Campus rules for Poway Unified were not checked."
- **Mods check** (`/mods`). Pick where the bike is ridden, any state, county, city, town, ZIP code or school district, then tick the changes made to it, like a speed unlock, a bigger motor or a passenger seat. Each one gets a YES or NO from the rules engine for that state and those places, with the reason. In California each answer also shows the penalties, the law and the changes that start Jan 1, 2027.
- **Find a bike** (`/find`). A short quiz on the rider's age and height, where they ride, what the bike is for, the budget and colors. It lists 29 bikes read from their makers' pages, ranked by fit alone, each with a YES or NO from the rules engine for this rider in these places.
- **Map** (`/map`). Bike racks to lock up an e-bike, bike shops and repair stations, from OpenStreetMap, for the part of the map on screen. Search a place or tap "Use my location", turn racks, shops or repair stations on and off with the chips over the map, then tap a mark or a row in the nearest list, and the two stay in step. Crowded marks join into square bubbles with a count. After the first look the map waits for "Search this area" instead of asking on every move. `docs/MAP_DATA.md` lists the sources, licenses and limits.

Built for Neighborhood Hacks 2026 (Oct 16 to 23, 2026).

## Why

The City of San Diego could start citing riders under its new e-bike rules on Oct 12, 2026, after 60 days of warnings. Riders under 12 can no longer ride Class 1 or 2 e-bikes there, SDUHSD now admits only tagged Class 1 and 2 e-bikes, and stores sell pedal-less e-motos under "kids e-bikes." Families have to sort out state law, city law and school rules for one specific bike. Guides explain the rules. This gives a verdict for one bike and one rider.

## Where they ride

One search box, "Where do they ride?", finds any US state, county, city, town, community or public school district. It reads one file, `public/places/us-places.json`, built from the Census Gazetteer files (public domain), and loads it from the app's own origin the first time the box is used. The file has 41,766 rows: 3,144 counties, 32,071 places and 6,551 towns and townships. The 51 states and DC, the City of San Diego's 52 community planning areas, and 71 everyday neighborhood names that City of San Diego pages give for parts of the City, like Hillcrest, University City, Point Loma, Scripps Ranch and Del Mar Heights, are added in code. The short names the City Council district pages use, like Clairemont and San Pasqual, find their planning areas too. Every result shows its county and state, so Carmel Valley in San Diego cannot be confused with Carmel Valley Village in Monterey County. `docs/PLACES_DATA.md` lists the sources, their dates and how to rebuild the file with `scripts/build-places.ts`. ZIP codes load from `public/places/us-zips.json`, built by `scripts/build-zips.ts` from the Census 2020 ZCTA relationship files, and `docs/PLACES_DATA.md` gives the rule each ZIP follows.

- A pick maps by its Census code, state and county, never by name alone (`lib/places/resolve.ts`). The five checked cities map to their own records. The City of San Diego's community planning areas, like Carmel Valley and La Jolla, and its everyday neighborhood names, like Hillcrest and Del Sur, map to the City. San Diego County and its unincorporated communities map to the unincorporated county record, except three that are mostly base or reservation land, which get California law only.
- Every other place uses its state's verified law, and the answer says so in plain words, like "City and county rules for Austin were not checked."
- Several places can be picked as chips, all in one state, since one answer covers one state's law. Picking a place in another state starts over there and says so in a status line. Each chip has a remove button named for the whole place.
- Exact names come first, then names that only start that way, so "Boston" lists Boston, Massachusetts before Bostonia in San Diego County, and "Kent" lists Kent, Washington before Kentucky. A checked San Diego place comes first among names that match the same way.
- The search is an ARIA 1.2 combobox. The arrow keys move through the results and Enter picks the highlighted one. With none highlighted, Enter picks the one place the name can only mean in the state the answer uses, like Encinitas, or else highlights the first result, so a place the family hasn't seen is never picked for them, and one key press never moves a San Diego ride to Miramar, Florida. Escape closes the list and then clears the box, and a screen reader hears how many places matched. No match, loading and a failed load are shown under the box too. The list opens again when the box is tapped or focused again, or on a phone's Search key. A state still being typed, like "Encinitas, Cali" or "Austin Tex", and a Saint, Mount or Fort still being typed, like "Sain", keep the results on screen. It works in English and Spanish, and Spanish results and chips name counties in Spanish, like "condado de San Diego".
- Removing a chip moves keyboard focus to the next chip, or with none left, back to the box, or on a phone to the box's label, so the phone's keyboard doesn't pop up. A tap on a chip's remove button right after a pick does nothing, so a quick second tap can't remove the place just added.
- A pick in another state is shown in a status line on the page, like "Added Austin, Travis County and 3 other counties, TX. The answer now uses Texas law.", along with the SDUHSD campus choice it removes.
- Every operating public school district in the 50 states and DC, 19,430 of them, comes from the NCES Common Core of Data directory for school year 2025-26 (preliminary, data as of May 6, 2026) and the 2024-25 EDGE district geocodes, read Oct 21, 2026. They load from `public/places/us-districts.json` with the place index. If that file fails to load, cities and counties still work. Each result reads "School district", its office city, county and state, and its NCES type when it is not a regular district, like "charter district", so the three Jefferson Elementary districts in California can't be confused. Two districts that match in every one of those, like the two Excel Academy Charter Districts in Irvine, also show their NCES ID. Typing a district's office city after a comma, like "San Dieguito Union High, Encinitas", finds it. A district maps by its NCES ID only. San Dieguito Union High, ID 0634380, ticks the SDUHSD campus choice instead of adding a chip. Every other district adds a chip and uses its state's law, and the answer says "Campus rules for Poway Unified were not checked." `scripts/build-districts.mjs` rebuilds the file, and `docs/PLACES_DATA.md` lists the sources and caveats.
- The SDUHSD campus stays its own choice, shown when the ride is in San Diego County. A plain state picker stays as a fallback. Old links like the QR flyer's `?state=CA&place=city_of_san_diego,sduhsd_campuses` still work, and map to the same entries as the search.

## How it decides

The AI only reads. A fixed rules table decides.

1. **Reading the bike.** A label photo is read by Tesseract OCR right in the phone's browser, so the photo never leaves the phone. A product link is fetched once on the server. Then a fixed-rules parser pulls out the class, watts, speeds, throttle and pedals. It reports a value only when it finds it written in the text, and it shows the exact words it used. It skips the other products under a heading like "You may also like" or "Frequently bought together", and reads this bike's own spec table again at a heading like "Specifications" or "Product information". It leaves the throttle blank when the text says both "no throttle" and "thumb throttle", and never reads "no pedals" wording as pedals. When the text says the pedals were removed, are missing or broken, aren't needed, are optional or an add-on, or don't ship, or answers "Pedals:" with anything but a clear yes, the pedals answer is left to the family. "No foot pegs" reads as no foot pegs, and optional or mixed foot-peg wording is left to the family. A number glued to a letter, the way OCR prints "l500W" for 1500 W, is never read from its later digits. A number split by a space, like "1 500 W" or an OCR "2 8 MPH", is not read at all, and the family is told to check it. It skips battery, motor, box and payload weights, and a weight listed without or before the battery. A weight given with the battery in the next clause is compared with the first one. When weights or wheel sizes disagree, or a size only appears next to an upgrade kit, it uses none and the family is asked. A trike word in a title or slogan never fills in the number of wheels. If the user isn't sure whether the bike has pedals, an optional photo check can look at a side photo of the whole bike. It runs a free detector, OWLv2, on the phone with WebGPU, downloads 194 MB once and only after the user agrees, and only fills the pedals answer. On a close call it asks instead.
2. **Checking the facts.** The "Check the facts" step shows every fact so the user can fix it before anything is decided, including the wheel size, number of wheels and weight that some state rules turn on. A number out of range, like a 57 typed from a tire's millimeter size, counts as not known.
   - Every fact read from a product link, pasted text, a label photo or the pedal photo check gets one soft row under it: a short mark, "Read from the text" (or "Read by the photo check"), and the words it came from, so the family sees where it came from and can change it. Once the family changes a fact, the row gives way to "Read from" and the same words. There is no box to tick. A fact read from a link or photo counts the same as a fact typed in, since the reader only reports what it finds written and shows the words it used. The number boxes are text boxes with a number keypad, not number inputs, so a mouse wheel, a trackpad swipe or an arrow key can't change a number nobody typed. In a radio group the arrow keys pick each answer they pass, so the throttle speed is cleared only while the throttle answer is No, and moving off No puts it back. Two different wheel sizes share one row under both sizes. Once the card shows Front and Rear boxes it keeps them, so a size midway through typing, like the "1" of "18", never removes the box being typed in. The unlock fact reads "It has an unlock or off-road mode", so it describes the bike, not the text. After a read, keyboard focus moves to the "Check the facts" heading, unless the family moved to another box while the label or link was read, like the rider's age or the paste box, so their typing is never cut off. A screen reader hears how many facts were read and that each one shows the words it was read from. That line is cleared once the family edits a fact or switches the language, so it is never read out again after it stops being true.
   - A new link, text or label photo is a new bike. A pedal photo call that changes the pedals answer marks it "Read by the photo check" and shows the text's words as what the text said before the photo check. A close call leaves the answer as it was. The photo check's message is picked when the call lands and cleared when the family picks a pedals answer, so a screen reader never hears "the answer above is now no" after they picked Yes. A call that lands after the family picked a pedals answer while the check ran is dropped, and the message says so. A pedals answer picked before the check started is replaced by the photo's call, since the family asked for the check. A call is applied only to the facts on screen as it lands, so a call for the last bike that lands just as a new link or label is read is dropped. The photo check says what it is doing and what it found on one status line that is always on the page, so a screen reader reads out the result. Each step of the photo check moves keyboard focus to its first control, and the photo input keeps focus while the photo is checked, so focus never drops to the page. A family that moved on to another fact while the photo was checked keeps their place. A language switch clears the photo check's message, the same as the read count. An edit is applied only to the facts it was made on. If a new link, label or photo call replaces them before the page redraws, the edit is dropped, so it never lands on a bike the family hasn't seen. Nothing is saved, so a reload or a shared link starts with no facts. The logic is in `lib/provenance.ts`, apart from the page, so it is tested on its own. The page's own handlers, state and effects are tested too, through a small stand-in for React's renderer in `tests/hooks.ts`. That includes the new key each read gives the facts card, which takes the last bike's photo check off the page.
3. **Deciding.** A deterministic TypeScript engine (`lib/rules/`) applies the rules table in `data/rules/`. Every sentence on a verdict card comes from a template in `lib/i18n/verdicts.ts`, in both languages. No model writes legal text.
4. **Answering YES or NO.** Every place card and the bottom line answer a plain YES or NO, never a maybe. Only an allowed card with nothing held back is YES. An allowed card says NO, with the same reason as the bottom line, when the bottom line can't confirm that place: a "not sure" fast-mode answer, a record not read in enacted text, or a ride after the state check runs out. The rule the card rests on stays on the card, under "What the rule says for these facts". If a fact the law depends on is missing, the engine asks a quick follow-up question (wheel size, fast modes, weight), and the card says NO until it is answered. A "Not sure" answer, a law that doesn't clearly cover the bike, or a fact the rules can't confirm is NO too, with a plain reason under it, like "We can't confirm it's legal in South Carolina." It never guesses toward "legal." Inside, the engine still keeps can't confirm apart from not allowed, so the bottom line can say exactly what is missing. Only the words changed, not what the engine decides.
5. **The bottom line.** Under every card, one strict YES or NO covers the whole ride: state law, every San Diego place picked, and the SDUHSD campus if picked, on the ride date. The rule lives in `lib/rules/bottomLine.ts`, apart from the page, so it is tested on its own.
   - **YES** only if every card says allowed, every follow-up question is answered (a "not sure" fast-mode answer counts as open, and a Class 1 or 3 label needs a clear "no throttle"), every rule behind the cards was read in enacted text, and the ride is no more than 30 days after the state rules were checked. That means statute-verified state rules and `primary_verified` San Diego records. It lists what the rider must still do, merged with duplicates removed, and says it covers streets and bike lanes, since sidewalks, parks, beaches and trails have their own rules. When a picked place's own city or county rules were not checked, the card says so in bold, and so does the sentence a screen reader hears, like "City and county rules for Austin were not checked."
   - **NO, not allowed** if any card says not allowed or not an e-bike. It names each place and the reason. When every such card only says not an e-bike, the line under NO says that instead, since the machine may be legal as another vehicle, like a Vermont motor-assisted bicycle, and the app does not check those rules.
   - **NO, can't confirm** if nothing is illegal but something is open, can't be confirmed, or rests on rules that weren't read in enacted text. The headline is a plain NO. The line under it names the one place the NO rests on, like "We can't confirm it's legal on an SDUHSD campus.", or says every place we checked when there are more. Under What's missing it says exactly what is missing, each item in one short line, like "Rules confirmed only through Nov 15, 2026.", with the full sentence folded under it, in both languages. Each place card shows its own YES or NO, and a card never says YES when the bottom line's NO rests on that card. A San Diego place card's YES rests on state law too, so on a ride date past the state check it says NO along with the state card. Uncertainty never becomes a YES.
   - No place is picked unless a QR code or shared link names one, so the places an answer covers are always the family's own pick. If no city or county is picked, a picked place has no checked record, or the state has no local layer, the answer says those rules were not checked, and names the picked places when there are any. There is never a bare YES for an unchecked place.
   - Local rules end on their own dates. The City of San Diego's under-12 ban ends Jan 1, 2029 with the state law behind it, unless the state extends that law. Carlsbad's code gives its ban no end date, so from that day a Carlsbad answer for a rider under 12 is NO, can't confirm, never YES. Poway and San Marcos are the same. Escondido's ban takes effect Nov 15, 2026 by its own text, but the state default for a general law city would have started it Oct 16, 2026, so between those dates an Escondido answer for a rider under 12 is NO, can't confirm.
   - A place with no under-12 ban in force vouches only for ride dates up to 30 days after its records were checked. A ban can come by ordinance, or by a resolution that takes effect the day it is adopted, but state law (VC 21214.7(e)) first requires a public information campaign of at least 30 days, and each record's city or County news was read for one. For a rider under 12 on a Class 1 or 2, a later date is NO, can't confirm, never YES. That includes the City of San Diego after its ban ends.
   - State law gets the same 30-day horizon. The state table was checked on Oct 16, 2026, so a ride after Nov 15, 2026 is NO, can't confirm until the table is checked again, since a new law could be in force by then. The state card says NO too, with the same line. A rule that says no still says no.
   - The ride date can be today through Dec 31, 2029. The rules table keeps only the rules in force from its check on, so a typed past date gets an error, not an answer, and a device clock that runs behind can't reach a date before the check. "Today" is read again at midnight and whenever the page comes back into view, so a tab left open overnight never answers for yesterday.

The San Diego records are held to a reference standard. On Oct 19, 2026 an independent skeptic pass reread the first seven against enacted ordinance, code, resolution, board and statute text, and only changes it confirmed from that text went in. On Oct 20, 2026 thirteen more cities were added: Chula Vista, Coronado, El Cajon, Escondido, Imperial Beach, La Mesa, Lemon Grove, National City, Oceanside, Poway, San Marcos, Santee and Vista. A first reader found each city's code sections, ordinances and council records, and a second, independent reader reread every cited section before a record went in. Seven of the thirteen have adopted an under-12 ban (Chula Vista, Coronado, Escondido, La Mesa, Poway, San Marcos and Santee, with Escondido's taking effect Nov 15, 2026) and six have none. Each record in `data/rules/san_diego_local.json` carries a `verification_level` and its `primary_sources` (number, link, effective date and quote). Nineteen places are `primary_verified`. SDUHSD is `primary_partial`. Its helmet rule is in Administrative Regulation 5142, but its class, tag, course, walk-on-campus, passenger-seat and e-moto rules are only on the district's web page, and the Board has not voted on the class, tag and course rules, so a campus answer can be NO, not allowed, but never YES.

The costly mistake is calling a bike legal when it isn't, so the engine is built around that:

- Never "legal" in Alaska, Kentucky or South Carolina, where the law doesn't clearly cover e-bikes.
- The watt line is read per state. A 750 W label passes in the 24 states that say "750 watts or less" and is NO, can't confirm, in the 19 that say "less than 750."
- Every `verdict_cautions` condition in the table is checked, with three-valued logic, so a fact the label can't show becomes a question instead of a guess.
- Rules switch on their effective dates (Hawaii Nov 12, 2026, North Carolina Dec 1, 2026, California, Colorado, Illinois, New Jersey and Oregon Jan 1, 2027, Utah May 5, 2027). Pick a future ride date to see them.
- A YES lists what the rider must still do. That includes a Class 3 speedometer in the 30 states whose records require one, New York's rule to ride only on roads posted 30 mph or less, and a parent or guardian, not any adult, for a Utah rider 8 to 13.
- Class 2 verdicts carry a caution in the 18 states whose law also says the motor must stop when the rider stops pedaling. Reviewers kept Class 2 legal there, and so does this app, but no court has settled it.

## Mods check

`/mods` uses the checker's place search, so the family picks any state, county, city, town or school district, with the same rules: one state at a time, a pick in another state starts over there, San Dieguito Union High maps to the checked SDUHSD campus record, and any other district or unchecked city is named as not checked. Then the rider's age, the ride date, the bike's class before the mods and its rated watts now, and each mod. 18 mods are covered, from speed unlock kits and app unlocks to throttles, motors over 750 watts, bigger batteries, removed pedals, foot pegs, passenger and child seats, trailers, lights, horns, turn signals, wheels and tires, class labels, a disconnected brake cutoff and very high handlebars.

- Every state's answer comes from the rules engine. `lib/mods/facts.ts` turns the class, the mod and the family's answer about what it does now into bike facts: a speed unlock whose motor alone now passes 20 mph sets the motor-over-20 fact, pedal assist past 28 sets the assist-over-28 fact, a motor now over 750 W uses the rated watts typed in when they are over 750 and leaves them unknown otherwise, removed pedals set no pedals, and an added throttle sets a throttle. The bike before the mods has pedals and a throttle only for Class 2, since Class 1 and Class 3 have none. `lib/mods/ride.ts` runs the engine and its bottom line for that state, those places, the rider's age and the ride date, and the mod is YES only when the bottom line is YES. Otherwise it is NO with the engine's reason, like "We need the motor watts printed on the label." The engine's follow-up questions, like wheel size or fast modes, show in the bike step. Mods that change no fact the engine reads, like lights, a horn, a child seat, a trailer, a passenger seat, the label, the brake cutoff, handlebars and tires, keep the engine's answer for the bike as it is. The whole bike is YES only when every mod is YES and the engine says YES for all of them together, so a throttle and a 28 mph unlock on one Class 1 bike can't add up to a YES.
- In California every rule in `data/mods/ca-mods.json` applies too, exactly as before, so a California YES needs both. Outside California each card says in one line that the state's own rules for that mod and its penalties were not checked, and shows no penalties.
- Where the law turns on what the change does, like a speed unlock that lets the motor alone pass 20 mph, the family picks that, or "Something else, or not sure".
- In California, YES also needs the research to read the outcome as the same class or a new class, with no rule broken, like a Class 1 bike with a 20 mph throttle becoming Class 2, whose label must then be replaced. A mod that makes the bike Class 3 is NO for a rider under 16. Anything not an e-bike, not settled, unanswered or against a named rule is NO, with its reason, and the overall line keeps "stops it being legal" apart from "can't be confirmed" and "needs an answer". A bike that is no longer an e-bike is a motor vehicle that needs a license, registration and insurance.
- Each California answer shows the penalties word for word as the research gives them, the code sections with links and dates, and, for a ride on or after Jan 1, 2027, what SB 1167 and AB 2346 change. A rule's end date is its last day in effect, so VC 21214.5(b) and SDMC 84.21(b), repealed on Jan 1, 2029, last apply on Dec 31, 2028. The City of San Diego passenger rule and fine apply only when the City, or one of its neighborhoods, is picked, while the rule is in effect.
- The research in `data/mods/ca-mods.json` was read Oct 21, 2026 from the California Legislature's official bulk data (the leginfo pages refused automated reads), CHP, DMV and the City of San Diego code, and checked quote by quote by a second reader, who made 10 corrections. `data/mods/ca-mods.answers.json` says how the app reads each outcome, and `docs/MODS_DATA.md` lists the open questions. The legal text stays in English on the Spanish page, with a line above the answer that says so. The app's own lines about what each result means are in both languages.

## Find a bike

`/find` asks the rider's age and height, where they ride (the same place search), their main uses (school or commute, fun, trails, cargo or kids, folding), a budget and colors they like. It lists the catalog bikes in the budget.

- Each bike gets the rules engine's YES or NO for this rider in these places today, from the maker's stated specs only. A fact the page leaves out stays unknown, so a rule that needs it makes the answer NO, with the reason. A bike sold in more than one class is checked in every class it names, and a speed it can be set to counts as its top speed. A Class 3 mode next to a throttle is NO, since no maker page here says the throttle turns off in that mode. A pedal-less e-moto is never YES.
- "Check this bike" opens the checker with the maker's facts loaded as read facts, each with the maker's words it came from, so the family can change any that differ on the bike. The link carries every place picked, unchecked cities and school districts too, so the checker names the same places as not checked.
- Ranked by fit alone: a YES first, then the uses, colors and height that match, then the lower price, then a fixed tie-break from the bike's id. No maker's name counts for or against a bike, there are no images, and no link earns a fee. The NO bikes fold under one line below the YES bikes. The specs show only the speeds the maker states.
- `data/bikes/catalog.json` has 29 models from 11 makers, 25 e-bikes and 4 pedal-less e-motos, read from each maker's own page on Oct 21, 2026 and reopened field by field by a second reader the same day. Each shows its price as "on the maker's site Oct 21, 2026", its colors, key specs and the maker's link. `docs/BIKE_FINDER.md` has the details.

## Accuracy

See `RESULTS.md` and the in-app accuracy page, in English at `/accuracy` and Spanish at `/accuracy/es`. The headline: on 758,280 generated cases (every state, class, age band, caution condition and switch date, plus bikes with pedals unknown or missing, no class, two classes, facts that disagree, a throttle answered "not sure" with a throttle speed still stated, a throttle left blank, or a top speed of unknown mode), checked against an independent reading of the raw table in `tests/oracle.ts`, the engine gave **zero false-legal results** and never refused a case the table allows.

The bottom line has its own gates. On 1,003,920 generated rides, including every San Diego place alone, all of them and none, with and without a place whose own rules were not checked, it never said YES when the independent checker listed a reason against it, and never refused when it listed none. It said "NO, not allowed" exactly when the checker found a rule that says no. The line naming unchecked places is checked on the real rendered card, in both languages. Every rendered place card, bottom line and screen reader sentence the matrix reads says a plain YES or NO, in both languages, never a hedge like "Can't confirm", "unsure" or "not sure" in its place. No place card said YES under a NO that rests on that place, and every card's YES or NO matched what the raw data gives it, across 1,908 distinct rendered place cards in both languages. An allowed card says NO when the bottom line can't confirm it, like a ride date past the state check, so of the 338,230 allowed cards in the engine matrix, 54,678 say YES and 283,552 say NO, and `RESULTS.md` shows both. Facts read from a link or photo count the same as facts typed in, so the step where the family ticked each read fact, and its gates, were removed on Oct 22, 2026.

The school districts, the mods check and the bike finder have their own gates, all at 0 in `RESULTS.md`: no district but SDUHSD treated as checked, no mod YES beyond what the research says or with no answer picked, no reason, rule or penalty on the mods page that is not the research's own words, no mod YES in any state where the engine on the bike after the mod says anything but YES, no change to California's mods answers, and no catalog bike YES where the engine on the maker's facts alone says not allowed, across every state, 15 ages and the checked San Diego place sets.

Those tests prove the engine matches the table, not that the table matches the law. The state table was checked against statute text on Oct 16, 2026 in two verifier rounds (`data/rules/verification_report.md`, `data/rules/corrections.json`). The San Diego records were checked against enacted text on Oct 19 and Oct 20, 2026, as described above.

The pilot in `PILOT.md` set pass bars before each run. A free CLIP model caught every pedal-less e-moto but called 4 of 8 real e-bikes pedal-less, so whole-bike photos were cut from the app. A second, part-focused try was built on 47 photos and run once on 24 fresh ones. It caught only 9 of 12 no-pedal machines and got 18 of 24 overall against a bar of 22. A third try used OWLv2, a free open-vocabulary detector, with one rule picked on 71 photos. Before its single run on 24 fresh photos, the expected result was written down as 88.7%. It passed every bar, with 12 of 12 no-pedal machines and 23 of 24 overall, so whole-bike photos are back as an optional pedal check, never a verdict.

## Design

The dark header band links the checker, the mods check and the bike finder, and each keeps the page's language. One calm column, at most 640 px wide, in four steps: Where and who, The bike, Check the facts, The answer. Steps 3 and 4 show as outlines until they fill. While the page's script loads, a frame of the same shape shows, with the bar, the title, the tagline and the steps in the phone's language, so nothing jumps when the page arrives. A small inline script sets the page's language before the first paint (`lib/pageLang.ts`), which also tells screen readers the Spanish accuracy page is Spanish. The answer step leads with the bottom line YES or NO, then one compact row per place, whose rules, sources and dates open on tap. YES is green and NO is red, and each always comes with its mark and the word. The facts check is a list of each fact, with the words it was read from under it. On a phone the Check button stays at the bottom of the facts card. Body text is 16 px, tap targets are at least 44 px, source links included, and the light theme is the default, with a dark theme from the phone's setting. On cards "e-bike" and ages like "14-year-old" use non-breaking hyphens, so a line never ends on "e-". Headings and the flyer are balanced, and no paragraph leaves one word alone on its last line. Scrolling to a step is instant when the phone asks for reduced motion. `tests/contrast.test.ts` reads every color pair from `app/globals.css` and checks it against WCAG AA in both themes. The page makes no new outside requests, and Geist is bundled with the app.

## Privacy

No accounts and no database. Label photos are read on the device and never uploaded. The optional pedal photo check also runs on the device, and its model comes once from Hugging Face and jsDelivr. A product link is fetched once and nothing is stored. The listing route refuses private and internal addresses. The map sends a place search to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim, and asks our own server for the view on screen, which asks OpenStreetMap's Overpass servers and mirrors. It reads the device's location only when the user taps "Use my location". Nothing is stored beyond a cache of map areas.

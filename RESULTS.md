## Rules engine

The engine was checked on **758,280 generated cases**: every state and DC, every class, ten ages, 12 dates around each rule change and the 30-day re-check line, and 24 versions of the bike that each flip one fact a caution reads (watts, wheel size, three wheels, weight, foot pegs, pedal kit, fast modes, and unanswered or "not sure" answers). It also ran 17 bikes that are not one plain class: pedals unknown or missing, no class, two classes, facts that disagree, a throttle answered "not sure" with a throttle speed still stated, a throttle left blank with no speed, and a top speed of unknown mode on top of the assist speed. 213,600 of those are San Diego local cards.

An independent checker in `tests/oracle.ts` reads the raw JSON itself and lists every reason a case must not get a yes. It shares no code with the engine.

Every place card answers YES or NO. Only an allowed card is YES. A card that is not allowed, not an e-bike, can't be confirmed or still has a question open is NO, with a plain reason under it, like "We can't confirm it's legal in South Carolina." The engine decides exactly as before, only the words changed. The check renders 2,887 distinct place cards from these cases, each in English and Spanish, and fails any card whose headline is not YES or NO, whose mark or reason line doesn't match it, or that gives a hedge like "Can't confirm", "unsure" or "not sure" as the answer. The "Not sure" choice on a follow-up question is a question, not an answer, and it leads to NO.

An allowed card shows NO when the bottom line can't confirm that same card: a ride date past the 30-day state check, a "not sure" fast-mode answer, or a record not read in enacted text. 10 of the 12 dates in this matrix are past the state check for every state, so 83.8% of allowed cards show NO here. Every other verdict always shows NO.

Full test suite: 1042 of 1042 tests passed.

Mutation check, done once by hand on Oct 17. Turning off the never-legal rule, or reading "less than 750 W" as "at most 750 W", each made the false-legal test fail. So the gate can catch real mistakes.

**What this proves, and what it doesn't.** The tests prove the engine matches the rules table. They don't prove the table matches the law. The state table was checked against statute text on Oct 16, 2026 in two verifier rounds (`data/rules/verification_report.md`, `data/rules/corrections.json`). On Oct 19, 2026 an independent skeptic pass reread the first seven San Diego records against enacted ordinance, code, board and statute text. On Oct 20, 2026 thirteen more cities were added (Chula Vista, Coronado, El Cajon, Escondido, Imperial Beach, La Mesa, Lemon Grove, National City, Oceanside, Poway, San Marcos, Santee and Vista), each read in enacted code or ordinance text and then reread by an independent second pass. 19 of 20 records are `primary_verified`. SDUHSD is `primary_partial`, because its class, tag and course rules are only on the district's web page. Each record lists its primary sources in `data/rules/san_diego_local.json`. A Bike Coalition or police review of the table is still to do.

## Bottom line

Under every answer the app shows one strict YES or NO for the whole ride (`lib/rules/bottomLine.ts`). YES needs every card allowed, every question answered, no "not sure" fast-mode answer, every record read in enacted text, and a ride date no more than 30 days after the state rules were last checked. It was checked on **1,003,920 generated rides**: the same states, classes, bikes, ages and dates as above, and in California every place alone, all places together and none, each with and without a picked place whose own city or county rules were not checked (the old "Somewhere else" choice). A separate checker in `tests/oracle.ts` lists every reason a case must not get a YES, and every rule that says a ride is definitely not allowed. Facts read from a link or photo count the same as facts typed in. Each one shows the words it was read from on the facts card, so the family can see where it came from and change it. The caveat line is read from the real rendered card, 273 distinct cards in English and Spanish. Every bottom line, and the sentence screen readers hear, says a plain YES or NO first, with a plain reason under it, like "We can't confirm it's legal in Texas." That is read from 391 distinct rendered bottom lines, each in English and Spanish, with their sentences.

## Photo pilot v3 (a third try, preregistered in PILOT.md)

An open-vocabulary detector, OWLv2 (q4, 194 MB, run on WebGPU), scored each photo with one fixed rule: the best "chain" score, plus "bicycle", minus "motorcycle". The rule was picked on 71 dev photos and then run once on 24 fresh held-out photos. The expected result, stated before the run from the dev leave-one-out estimate, was 88.7% right with 29 of 31 no-pedal machines.

| Check | Bar | Result | Pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| No-pedal machines | All 12 called no pedals | 12 of 12 | Yes |
| Pedal call overall | At least 22 of 24 | 23 of 24 | Yes |
| Invented facts | Zero values with no label visible | 0 of 24 photos | Yes |

Hard cases 9 of 10, real-world photos 9 of 10, pedal bikes 11 of 12, about 6.1 seconds per photo on a Mac.

It passed, so whole-bike photos came back as an optional pedal sorter, never a verdict. It loads only when tapped, after showing the 194 MB download, it asks "Does it have pedals?" on close calls, and it only fills the pedals answer. One set of 24 photos can't pin accuracy closely (23 of 24 fits anything from 78.9% to 99.9%), so the dev estimate is the better guide to everyday accuracy.

## Rules engine

The engine was checked on **334,080 generated cases**: every state and DC, every class, ten ages, eight dates around each rule change, and 24 versions of the bike that each flip one fact a caution reads (watts, wheel size, three wheels, weight, foot pegs, pedal kit, fast modes, and unanswered or "not sure" answers). 40,320 of those are San Diego local cards.

An independent checker in `tests/oracle.ts` reads the raw JSON itself and lists every reason a case must not get a yes. It shares no code with the engine.

Mutation check, done once by hand on Oct 17. Turning off the never-legal rule, or reading "less than 750 W" as "at most 750 W", each made the false-legal test fail. So the gate can catch real mistakes.

**What this proves, and what it doesn't.** The tests prove the engine matches the rules table. They don't prove the table matches the law. The table was checked against statute text on Oct 16, 2026 in two verifier rounds (`data/rules/verification_report.md`, `data/rules/corrections.json`). The San Diego rows come from city and district pages, not ordinance text. A Bike Coalition or police review of the table is still to do.

## Photo pilot v3 (a third try, preregistered in PILOT.md)

An open-vocabulary detector, OWLv2 (q4, 194 MB, run on WebGPU), scored each photo with one fixed rule: the best "chain" score, plus "bicycle", minus "motorcycle". The rule was picked on 71 dev photos and then run once on 24 fresh held-out photos. The expected result, stated before the run from the dev leave-one-out estimate, was 88.7% right with 29 of 31 no-pedal machines.

| Check | Bar | Result | Pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| No-pedal machines | All 12 called no pedals | 12 of 12 | Yes |
| Pedal call overall | At least 22 of 24 | 23 of 24 | Yes |
| Invented facts | Zero values with no label visible | 0 of 24 photos | Yes |

Hard cases 9 of 10, real-world photos 9 of 10, pedal bikes 11 of 12, about 6.1 seconds per photo on a Mac.

It passed, so whole-bike photos came back as an optional pedal sorter, never a verdict. It loads only when tapped, after showing the 194 MB download, it asks "Does it have pedals?" on close calls, and it only fills the pedals answer. One set of 24 photos can't pin accuracy closely (23 of 24 fits anything from 78.9% to 99.9%), so the dev estimate is the better guide to everyday accuracy.
